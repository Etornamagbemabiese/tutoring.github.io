Global May 2026

Lessons from Cape Town: Consulting with Heart

I returned from an incredible experience in Cape Town, South Africa, through the partnership between UNC Kenan-Flagler and TSIBA Business School. This trip challenged me, inspired me, and reshaped the way I think about leadership, consulting, entrepreneurship, and human connection.

From the moment we arrived, the warmth and energy from the TSIBA students stood out immediately. Their compassion, hospitality, and sense of community reminded me how powerful intentional relationships and support systems can be.

One of the most impactful parts of the trip was working on a consulting project with social entrepreneur Lungi Joe, who runs a ceramics business while mentoring children in her community through art and creativity. Visiting her business and meeting the children she supports was inspiring. This project taught me that entrepreneurship can be about much more than profit; it can serve as a vehicle for empowerment and long-term community impact.

As consultants, our challenge was figuring out how to help her sustainably grow both the business and mentorship sides of her work. Through customer interviews, research, and testing ideas, I learned that good consulting is not about pushing your own solutions, but about listening deeply, understanding the root problem, and adapting based on evidence. Sometimes what a client initially wants may not be what best supports their long-term mission.

Beyond the consulting work, our wilderness retreat in the mountains was another defining experience. Disconnecting from technology and spending intentional time reflecting and connecting with the TSIBA students reminded me of the importance of slowing down and investing in people.

Most importantly, this experience showed me that leadership is not just about solving problems; it is about listening, building trust, and creating solutions that genuinely improve people's lives.

Leadership May 2026

Africa Night: A Sold-Out Finale

Africa Night was a powerful way to close out my tenure as Vice President of the UNC African Students Association — a completely sold-out event celebrating African culture, storytelling, and community on campus.

Alongside Joseph Warioba, Betsy George, and Oluwole Omolafe, I had the privilege of helping organize the event. Seeing the energy in the room, the pride in the performances, and the way people connected to the culture we were showcasing made all the late nights and planning meetings worth it. I also had the chance to step on stage and act in the play, which gave me a full-circle perspective on what it means to build something that others can experience and feel connected to.

As my final event as Vice President, Africa Night made me reflect on a few key lessons:

  • Leadership is rarely about doing everything yourself, and almost always about trust — trusting your team to execute, trusting the process when things feel uncertain, and trusting that the vision will come together in the end.
  • Impact is often measured in moments, not just outcomes. A sold-out venue is great, but what stood out more were the conversations, the pride, and the sense of belonging in the room.
  • So much can be accomplished when people come together with a shared purpose and genuine care for what they're building.

Grateful to everyone who made Africa Night possible and to the ASA community for an unforgettable year of growth and leadership.

Global April 2026

Inside Fido: Fintech & Financial Inclusion in Accra

I had the opportunity to visit Fido in Accra, and it was one of the most insightful experiences I've had exploring the fintech space in Africa.

What stood out most to me was how Fido is rethinking access to credit. In markets where traditional systems like FICO Score don't fully apply, Fido is leveraging alternative data to build a more inclusive financial ecosystem. Their proprietary "Fido Score" is helping make the financially excluded visible and bankable.

Beyond the product, I was equally impressed by the scale and impact: over 1 million customers across multiple African markets, strong partnerships with institutions like Access Bank and platforms like Uber, and a clear commitment to financial inclusion, innovation, and gender equity.

On the technical side, learning about their infrastructure — from machine learning-driven credit models to scalable backend systems — showed how deeply technology is embedded in solving real financial challenges.

But what I valued most was the conversation. Hearing directly from the team about career paths, the realities of building in emerging markets, and the importance of staying open-minded really stuck with me. One takeaway I'll carry forward: your career doesn't have to follow a straight line; what matters is continuously learning and being willing to explore new opportunities.

Venture Capital February 2026

VCIC South Regional at Rice: How Investors Really Think

I attended the VCIC South Regional at Rice University as a VCIC Fellow — an incredible experience that pushed my thinking about venture capital, investing, and founder evaluation.

One of my biggest takeaways was seeing how investors evaluate more than just metrics. Judges repeatedly emphasized that traction without clarity is fragile — strong numbers only matter if they reflect a repeatable and defensible business model. I also appreciated the strong focus on founder mindset, adaptability, and intellectual humility during due diligence conversations.

Another theme that stood out was how venture is evolving. Several judges discussed how traditional SaaS alone is becoming less compelling, with growing interest in startups built around proprietary data, AI integration, or deep industry workflows.

On a personal level, the experience reinforced my long-term interest in venture capital, especially in emerging markets like Africa, where investing requires contextual intelligence, strong judgment, and the ability to evaluate founders operating under real-world constraints.

Yale Africa Innovation Symposium

I attended the Yale Africa Innovation Symposium, a program dedicated to empowering African entrepreneurs who strive to create lasting change, as part of the UNC cohort.

I was assigned to a case study led by Augustine K. Degorl II, where we tackled the challenge of addressing educational disparities in Uganda. Our task was to design a hypothetical LMS system that could function offline while remaining accessible and equitable.

After an intense day of problem-solving and collaboration, we celebrated at a gala where King Promise delivered an incredible performance, adding to the unforgettable experience.
